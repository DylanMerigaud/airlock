# ASA rulings used as rule references

Two real rulings of the UK Advertising Standards Authority, both published 2026-08-26, read on
2026-08-28. Paraphrased here with the CAP Code rules they cite; the ASA pages are authoritative.

## A26-1337640: Nutri-Paw Ltd t/a Healthy Dog (upheld)

- URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/nutri-paw-ltd.html
- Ad: a paid Facebook video for a dog dental powder, fronted by a woman presented as a veterinary
  dental specialist, claiming routine anaesthesia kills healthy dogs and that the powder reduces
  tartar at home ("90 days later, 43 showed dramatic tartar reduction").
- CAP Code rules breached: 1.3 (social responsibility), 3.1 (misleading advertising),
  3.7 (substantiation), 12.11 (medicines, medical devices, health-related products).
- Why it matters for Airlock: an efficacy or health claim voiced by a presented expert, with no
  evidence behind it, is a BLOCK. This is the claim shape of the Crest commercial ("stops cavities",
  an organisation endorsement) and of the synthetic test clip.

## G26-1344778: Hotpoint UK Appliances Ltd (upheld)

- URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/hotpoint-uk-appliances-ltd.html
- Ad: a website headline "The UK's Most Trusted Appliance Brand" next to a Trustpilot score, the
  basis of the comparison (the count of five-star reviews among a filtered set of competitors) only
  visible on a click-through page.
- CAP Code rules breached: 3.1 and 3.3 (misleading advertising), 3.32 (comparisons with
  identifiable competitors).
- Why it matters for Airlock: a superlative or comparative claim ("#1", "most trusted", "better
  than") without its basis on the same screen is a BLOCK.

## How the claim gate cites them

The claim gate names the FTC section (16 CFR 255.x, `rules/ftc-16-cfr-255.md`) that a claim falls
under, and the ASA ruling above whose claim shape matches, as `rule_ids`. The gate does not decide
UK law; the ruling is the precedent a reviewer would pull, with its reference number.
