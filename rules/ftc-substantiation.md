# The advertiser's own claims: FTC Act section 5 and the substantiation doctrine

The Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, `rules/ftc-16-cfr-255.md`) govern what an endorser says
and who the endorser is. An advertiser's own efficacy, health, comparative or superlative claim
falls under the statute itself and the Commission's substantiation policy, cited here. Read on
2026-09-05; the sources are authoritative for wording, this file is a pointer.

## FTC Act section 5, 15 U.S.C. 45 (unfair or deceptive acts or practices)

- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/federal-trade-commission-act
- What it says, in two sentences: section 5 declares unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or
  affecting commerce unlawful and gives the Commission the authority to act against them. An
  advertising claim that is false, or that the advertiser had no reasonable basis for, is a
  deceptive practice under this section.
- Rule id in the claim gate: `FTC Act section 5 (15 U.S.C. 45)`.

## FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation (1983)

- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation
  (issued 1983; published as an appendix to Thompson Medical Co., 104 F.T.C. 648, 839 (1984), which
  is why the ftc.gov library dates the item 1984-11-23).
- What it says, in two sentences: an advertiser must possess and rely on a reasonable basis for an
  objective claim before the claim is disseminated, and lacking that basis is itself an unfair and
  deceptive act under section 5. What counts as a reasonable basis depends on the type of claim,
  the product, the consequences of a false claim, the benefits of a truthful one, the cost of
  developing substantiation and the amount of substantiation experts in the field would consider
  reasonable; an ad that states a level of proof ("clinically proven") must have at least that.
- Rule id in the claim gate: `FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation (1983)`.

## 16 CFR 14.15, comparative advertising

- Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/14.15 (eCFR: title 16, chapter I, subchapter A,
  part 14, section 14.15).
- What it says, in two sentences: the Commission's policy is that truthful comparative advertising
  that names competitors is lawful and useful to consumers, and it should not be restrained by
  broadcasters or industry codes. Comparative claims are held to the same substantiation standard
  as any other claim, not a higher one; disparagement of a competitor must not be false or
  deceptive.
- Rule id in the claim gate: `16 CFR 14.15`.

## Where the UK equivalents live

- CAP Code 3.7 (substantiation): marketers must hold documentary evidence for claims consumers
  are likely to regard as objective before the marketing communication runs. Rule id `CAP Code 3.7`.
- CAP Code 3.32 (comparisons with identifiable competitors): a comparison with an identifiable
  competitor must not mislead about either product; the ASA cited it in G26-1344778
  (`rules/asa-rulings.md`). Rule id `CAP Code 3.32`.
- CAP Code 2.1: a marketing communication must be obviously identifiable as such; the UK side of
  an undisclosed material connection (16 CFR 255.5 in the US). Rule id `CAP Code 2.1`.
- Source: https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html and
  https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html.

## What the gate does not decide

- Market: the gate cites the US and the UK rules on every regulated claim because it does not know
  where the asset airs. A reviewer keeps the column that applies.
- FDA: a cavity, decay or disease claim on toothpaste is also an over-the-counter drug claim under
  the anticaries monograph (21 CFR 355); the gate says so in the reason of a health claim and
  cites nothing further, the FDA rules are not in this repo.
- Puffery: subjective praise no reasonable viewer takes as a measurable fact ("the most exciting
  sound in town") needs no substantiation; the gate labels it advisory and cites nothing.
- Price: price claims (16 CFR 233, CAP Code 3.17 to 3.22) are labelled advisory and not read.
